Online

Messaging, video calls, and reading tone in text with an autistic person.

What might be happening

Text strips out tone of voice and facial expression, which can make sarcasm, jokes, or passive hints land very differently than intended. A short or very literal text reply usually isn't coldness, it's often just direct, efficient communication, and video calls can be tiring if they demand constant eye contact with a camera.

Try this

  • Say what you mean directly in messages, rather than relying on hints or sarcasm.
  • Use a clarifying emoji or "just kidding" if you're joking in text, tone doesn't always come through.
  • Be okay with someone looking slightly off-camera on a video call rather than at the lens.
  • Give a heads-up if you're switching from text to a call, rather than calling unannounced.

Avoid this

  • Reading a short or blunt text reply as annoyance.
  • Relying on sarcasm or heavy subtext in written messages.
  • Insisting someone keep their camera fixed on their face the whole call.
  • Cold-calling without warning when text would work fine.

A quick example

You send a joking, sarcastic text and get a very literal, confused reply back. Rather than assuming they're being difficult, a quick "Sorry, that was a joke!" clears it up fast, and adjusting your texting style for that person going forward avoids the same mix-up next time.
