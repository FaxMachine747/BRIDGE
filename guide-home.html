At Home

Family gatherings and everyday conversation with an autistic relative.

What might be happening

Family gatherings are often loud, crowded, and unpredictable, exactly the conditions that can make socialising harder for an autistic relative. If they step away from a busy room, decline a hug, or answer questions briefly, it's rarely about not caring, it's more often about managing sensory input or energy so they can keep participating at all.

Try this

  • Let them choose their own greeting, a wave or a handshake is fine if that's what they prefer.
  • Offer a quieter room or a break option at loud gatherings, without making it a big deal.
  • Give advance notice of plan changes when you can ("We're leaving at 6 instead of 7").
  • Ask direct, specific questions rather than broad ones ("How was the maths test?" rather than "How's school?").

Avoid this

  • Forcing physical affection or eye contact "for the photo."
  • Taking it personally if they leave a loud room to decompress.
  • Springing last-minute changes to plans without warning.
  • Comparing them to siblings or other relatives in front of the family.

A quick example

At a family dinner, a relative gets up and leaves the table mid-conversation. Instead of following them or asking why in front of everyone, let them go, and check in quietly later if you're concerned: "Just wanted to see how you're doing, no pressure to talk." This respects their need for space while keeping the door open.
