At Work

Meetings, small talk, and giving or receiving feedback with an autistic coworker.

What might be happening

An autistic coworker who skips the small talk before a meeting, asks for instructions in writing, or gives feedback very directly isn't being unfriendly or overly blunt on purpose. They're often communicating in the clearest way they know how, and treating that style as a working norm, rather than a flaw to correct, usually leads to a better working relationship.

Try this

  • Send meeting agendas or key questions in writing beforehand when you can.
  • Say what you need plainly ("Can you send that by Thursday?" rather than "No rush, whenever").
  • Treat direct feedback as useful information, not as rudeness.
  • Ask how they prefer to receive feedback, in writing, in person, or in a 1:1, rather than assuming.

Avoid this

  • Relying only on vague verbal instructions given in passing.
  • Reading a lack of small talk as coldness or disinterest in the team.
  • Softening feedback so much that the actual point gets lost.
  • Assuming they dislike collaboration because they prefer written communication first.

A quick example

A coworker gives you very direct feedback on a draft: "This section is confusing, rewrite it." Rather than reading this as harsh, treat it as clear, actionable information, ask a specific follow-up like "Which part specifically?" This gets you a useful answer faster than a vaguer, softer request would have.
