In Public

Shops, transport, and unplanned interactions with autistic strangers.

What might be happening

Public spaces are full of noise, bright lights, and unpredictable people, which can be genuinely overwhelming for an autistic person you don't know. If someone seems distressed, avoids interaction, or responds unusually to a simple question in a shop or on transport, patience and a bit of space usually help more than trying to fix the situation for them.

Try this

  • Keep instructions or questions short and specific ("The exit is on your left" rather than a long explanation).
  • Give people space if they seem overwhelmed, rather than crowding in to help.
  • If someone seems distressed, a calm, quiet "Are you okay, do you need anything?" is usually enough.
  • Be patient with unusual movements or sounds (stimming), they're not a threat.

Avoid this

  • Staring at or commenting on stimming or unusual behaviour.
  • Physically approaching or touching someone who looks overwhelmed without asking first.
  • Assuming a lack of response to a question means someone is being rude.
  • Raising your voice if you're not understood the first time.

A quick example

Someone in a checkout queue seems distressed by the noise and isn't responding to the cashier. Rather than speaking for them or getting impatient, giving a moment of quiet space, and a simple, calm question directed at them, "Take your time," usually helps more than repeating instructions louder or faster.
