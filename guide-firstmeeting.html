First Meeting

Making a good first impression with an autistic person, without guesswork.

What might be happening

First meetings come with a lot of unwritten social rules, handshakes, small talk, how much eye contact is "normal", that aren't always intuitive or comfortable for an autistic person. Getting a slightly different greeting or a more direct introduction than you expected isn't a bad sign, it's just a different starting style, and it's easy to build a good first impression around it.

Try this

  • Introduce yourself simply and clearly ("Hi, I'm Alex, nice to meet you.").
  • Let them lead on physical greetings, a handshake, wave, or nod are all fine.
  • Ask direct, specific questions to get a conversation going ("What are you working on at the moment?").
  • Be patient with pauses, they don't mean the conversation is going badly.

Avoid this

  • Reading a shorter or more formal introduction as unfriendliness.
  • Initiating a hug or handshake without a clear cue that it's welcome.
  • Filling every pause immediately, some people just need a beat longer.
  • Deciding within the first minute that the meeting "went badly."

A quick example

You meet someone new and they give a brief, formal introduction and don't offer much small talk. Instead of assuming disinterest, ask one direct, specific question about something concrete, a shared class, project, or event. A focused question often gets a much fuller, more comfortable response than an open "so, tell me about yourself."
