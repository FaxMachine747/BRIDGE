At School

Group work, classroom conversation, and lunchtime interactions with an autistic classmate.

What might be happening

If a classmate goes quiet during group work, avoids eye contact, or gives short, very direct answers, it usually isn't a sign they're uninterested or being difficult. It's more likely they're processing the conversation in a way that just looks different from what you're used to, and small adjustments from you can make the interaction work better for both of you.

Try this

  • Give a few extra seconds of silence after asking a question before repeating it.
  • Say clearly what you mean rather than hinting ("Can you pass the notes?" rather than "It's a bit far from you, isn't it").
  • Let them opt out of eye contact, keep talking normally.
  • If group work feels overwhelming for them, offer a smaller task within the group.

Avoid this

  • Repeating or rephrasing a question immediately after asking it.
  • Assuming silence or a flat tone means they're annoyed at you.
  • Insisting on eye contact or commenting on the lack of it.
  • Speaking about them to others as if they're not there.

A quick example

You ask a classmate, "Do you want to work on the intro or the conclusion?" and they don't answer right away. Instead of repeating the question or picking for them, wait a few seconds. If they still seem unsure, offer a concrete choice: "I can start the intro, do you want to take the conclusion?" This gives them a clear, specific option to respond to, rather than an open-ended question.
