About Bridge

Why this exists, and where the guidance comes from.

Why Bridge exists

Most communication advice about autism is written for parents, teachers, or clinicians. Very little is written for the people who actually need it in the moment, classmates, coworkers, and friends who want to get it right but don't know where to start. Bridge is built for that gap: short, scenario-based guidance instead of long clinical articles.

Bridge is also built on one core idea from current research: communication breakdowns between autistic and non-autistic people go both ways. So instead of another resource asking autistic people to mask or adapt, Bridge is aimed at the non-autistic side of the conversation.

Sources

The guidance on this site is based on the following research and organisations:

  • Milton, D. "On the Ontological Status of Autism: The 'Double Empathy Problem.'" Disability & Society, vol. 27, no. 6, 2012, pp. 883–887.
  • Crompton, C. J., Ropar, D., Evans-Williams, C. V., Flynn, E. G., & Fletcher-Watson, S. "Autistic Peer-to-Peer Information Transfer Is Highly Effective." Autism, vol. 24, no. 7, 2020, pp. 1704–1712.
  • Edey, R., Cook, J., Brewer, R., Johnson, M. H., Bird, G., & Press, C. "Interaction Takes Two: Typical Adults Exhibit Mind-Blindness Towards Those with Autism Spectrum Disorder." Journal of Abnormal Psychology, vol. 125, no. 6, 2016, p. 879.
  • "Showing Neurotypicals How Autistic Communication Works." Tufts Now, 5 Dec. 2025.
  • "Communication and Autism." National Autistic Society.
  • Cognitive Accessibility Guidelines (COGA), World Wide Web Consortium (W3C).

Full citation details are available in the accompanying project report.

A note on this build

This site was built as part of an MYP Design project (Criterion C). The video sections are placeholders, in a full production version these would be short, captioned clips filmed with real volunteers. All six scenario guides, the myth-busting page, and the self-check quiz are fully working.

Bridge is a student MYP Design project.
